The History of Blogging: When and How It All Started

Published on: July 29, 2026

What Exactly is a Blog?

A "blog" (a shortened version of "weblog") is an online journal or informational website displaying information in reverse chronological order, with the latest posts appearing first. It is a platform where a writer or a group of writers share their views, tutorials, or daily lives on an individual subject.

The Early Days: 1994

The concept of blogging was born in the mid-1990s. In 1994, a college student named Justin Hall created what is widely considered the very first blog, Links.net. It wasn't called a blog at the time—it was just a personal homepage where he shared links and thoughts, manually coding the HTML for every single entry.

Where Did the Word Come From?

The actual term "weblog" was coined in 1997 by Jorn Barger to describe the process of "logging the web" as he surfed the internet. Two years later, in 1999, a programmer named Peter Merholz jokingly broke the word into the phrase "we blog" in the sidebar of his website. The term stuck, and the modern word "blog" was officially born.

The Boom: No More Manual Coding

Before 1999, you had to know how to write code and upload files via FTP to run a blog. That changed completely when a company called Pyra Labs launched a platform called Blogger. It allowed anyone, regardless of technical skill, to type into a text box and publish their thoughts to the internet instantly. (Google later bought Blogger in 2003).

In 2003, WordPress launched, which fundamentally changed the internet. It gave users total control over their designs and content. Today, WordPress powers over 40% of all websites on the entire internet.

Why Do People Blog Today?

What started as a digital diary has evolved into a massive, multi-billion dollar digital industry. Today, people and businesses blog to:

  • Share Knowledge: Educate others and establish authority in a specific field.
  • Build Communities: Connect with like-minded people across the globe.
  • Drive Business: Boost Search Engine Optimization (SEO) to get corporate websites to the top of Google.
  • Monetize: Earn money through display ads, affiliate marketing, and sponsorships.

From humble beginnings of hand-coded text files to the foundation of modern media, the blog remains one of the most powerful tools for communication on the internet.
